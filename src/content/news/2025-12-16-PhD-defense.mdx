I defended my PhD thesis at [CNRS-AIST Joint Robotics Laboratory](https://jrl.cnrs.fr).

{/* more */}

My thesis, _Sustained Physical Human-Robot Interaction: From Human-Inspired
Motions to Safe, Adaptable, Precise Control toward Human-Centered Industry_,
brings together three levels of the interaction problem.

It reconsiders minimum-jerk motion as an online, disturbance-aware reference;
introduces explicit task- and direction-level compliance inside an
inverse-dynamics quadratic program; and validates the resulting control framework
on a torque-controlled manipulator under sustained physical contact.

The defense closed my doctoral work and opened the next research question: how a
robot can use interaction history to identify its partner's role and learn to act
collaboratively.
